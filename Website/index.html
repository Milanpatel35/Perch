How it works

Open it once.It does the rest.

01.

One app. One launch.

Perch is a single native Swift app. No account, no setup wizard, no Electron runtime sitting in your menu bar. Open it once and an island settles in around the notch. That's the whole setup.

02.

Nothing you didn't allow.

Each module asks for its own macOS permission the first time you switch it on — the calendar for your next meeting, accessibility for the volume overlay. Grant what you want, skip the rest. Nothing is requested at launch.

03.

Your day, in the notch.

Play a song, join a call, copy a link, park a file. Each one becomes a live activity above the keyboard. Glance to check it, hover to peek, click to expand — right where your eyes already are.

04.

Then it disappears.

When nothing needs you, the island goes quiet: a black sliver shaped like the notch it lives in, doing no work at all. Status flashes surface for a second, then fade. You stop noticing it's running. That's the point.

FAQ

Asked, answered.

Is there a Dynamic Island for Mac?
Not natively — no Mac ships one, it's an iPhone feature. Perch draws an expandable black panel around your camera housing so the notch behaves like one: live activities for music, calendar, calls and AirPods, plus a shelf and clipboard history.
Is it really free, or free until version 2?
Really free. There's no Pro tier planned and no licence-key code in the repository. And because it's MIT licensed, if that ever changed anyone could fork the last free build and keep it going.
Does it work on Macs without a notch, or on external displays?
Yes. On notchless Macs and external displays Perch runs as a floating island at the top of the screen. It never draws a fake notch over your wallpaper.
Will it drain my battery?
It shouldn't. The cost in notch apps comes from audio visualisers, constantly polled system statistics, and anything needing screen recording. Perch is event-driven throughout, and a module that's switched off schedules no work at all. Check Activity Monitor after an hour if you want to verify.
Why macOS 13 when everyone else needs 14?
Because a lot of perfectly good Macs stopped at Ventura, and those are exactly the machines whose owners don't want to spend $25 on a notch utility. Nearly every paid app in this category requires Sonoma or later.
Can I run it alongside another notch app?
You can install both, but don't expect it to go well. They compete for the same screen region and the same hover and click targets. Pick one.
Is it safe to install?
Perch is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarised, so Gatekeeper opens it without a warning. Clips, files and calendar events stay in the app's own container and are never uploaded. The only network request it makes is to its update feed. If you'd rather check yourself, the source is right here.
